values (`amexCheckingScenario` S4, S5). D3 (the Amex anchor) is B5's and is untouched.

## The refund rules, in plain words

**What a refund is** (`classifyRefund`, `lib/avalanche-core/src/spendingRule.ts`). Money coming back on an
account the household buys on, that is not a transfer, not income and not a card payment:

- **On the card's own ledger** (`amex`, `plaid:amex`), every credit is money back, filed or not — unless
  something below rules it out.
- **On any other account** (checking, a manual row) the description must say so: the word REFUND, REFUNDS,
  REFUNDED or RFND, and no tax word (TAX, IRS, TREAS). An unfiled paycheck, an ACH return, a "TARGET
  RETURN" or a transfer in is never netted. Missing a refund reads low; netting a deposit would read high.
- **Never a refund:** a transfer; a debt row (`debtId`, a debt category); the card-payment flag; an
  excluded category (Transfer, Ignore, Reimbursement, …); an income category; Plaid's card-payment,
  transfer, loan-payment or income categories (a tax refund included); a card-payment or bank-noise
  description; a description that reads as a payment ("ONLINE PAYMENT - THANK YOU", "AUTOPAY", "PYMT").
- **Reimbursable:** a reimbursable refund nets what the card owes (the card owes less whoever is paid
  back) but never household spending, which never counted the reimbursable charge. This is rule 7's mirror.

**Where it nets.** A refund nets on **its own account**, inside **the window it is dated in**, and **never
below zero** there. It never reaches another account's spending or another window.

- **Household spending** (`buildSpendingFacts`): this is the spine's `spentWeek` / `spentMonth` and the
  Spending report.
  - Per account: `max(0, purchases − refunds)`.
  - A refund **filed to a category** also nets that category on that account: `realSpend` and `byCategory`.
  - The breakdowns by merchant, day, weekday and month stay purchases.
  - A new `refunds` field reports what was netted. Then
    `householdSpend = realSpend + uncategorized − (refunds.total − refunds.fromCategories)`.
  - `classifierHouseholdSpend` nets the same way, so the parity tests still hold.
- **The allowance:** the money position (`spentWeekDiscretionary`, `needsClassificationWeek`,
  `remainingWeek`, `safeToSpendNow`), the hooks' remaining allowance and the daily metrics'
  `discretionaryWtd` / `discretionaryMtd`.
  - `classifyMovement` gives a refund the coverage `refund`, plus `nets`: the coverage a purchase with the
    same flags and matches would have.
  - `allowanceTotals` nets per account, in three pools, each floored at zero:
    - the cap's pool = weekly + unfiled (both count against the cap);
    - unplanned;
    - monthly.
  - The unfiled figure is never more than the cap's pool.
- **The card** (`computeWeeklyPayoff`): the Amex page's `weekCharges` and the hooks' charges are the
  card's charges less its refunds in the card's window, filed or not, never below zero. `chargeCount`
  and `topMerchant` stay the charges.

**Why the cap's weekly and unfiled amounts net as ONE pool.** The open week's payoff is
`card charges + (cap − spent)`.

- A refund takes its amount off the card's charges whatever flag it carries.
- The room must give back exactly as much, or the payoff would shrink while the room stayed put. A
  smaller payoff means a higher curve: it would read high.
- Example: a weekly-filed $50 charge, refunded by an unfiled $30 credit.
  - Floored as two pools, it would pay off $20 + (cap − 50).
  - Floored as one pool, it pays off $20 + (cap − 20) = the cap.
  - The test is `refunds.test.ts`, "a weekly-filed charge refunded by an unfiled credit still holds"; mutant M2 kills the two-pool version.

**Timing.** A refund counts in the window it is **dated** in.

- A refund dated after a week closed nets the **next** week's charges and allowance.
- It never re-opens or re-sizes the closed week. That week's payoff stays its charges, and the payment
  evidence that paid it is untouched.
- This is tested by `refunds.test.ts`, "a refund on a card after the week closed nets the NEXT week",
  and by `refundsNet` week 2.

**The decision (D2).** Linking happens in the categorizer, `stages/heuristic.ts`.

- **Signature.** Refund linking keys on `refundSignature`. This is `merchantSignature` without the words
  REFUND / CREDIT / RETURN / RFND, and their plurals and past tenses, removed as whole words. Both
  "KROGER #442 REFUND" and "KROGER #442" sign `kroger`. Both sides of the link use it.
- **Which purchase.** A credit that nothing above rules out links to an earlier purchase that:
  - has the same signature;
  - is dated on or before it, at most **90 days** before (was 60);
  - is at least as large.

  The credit's **own account** is preferred, then the latest.
- **Its decision.** It gets a **queue** decision (0.55), `source: "refund"`, that proposes the purchase's
  category. The explanation is **"Refund of <purchase> on <date>"** and `refund_of_txn_id` is set.
- **Stage order.** It runs right after `locked`. Memory, a mapping rule or a recurring item can never
  auto-file a linked refund as the purchase it returns. Only a replaced pending row's filing still
  carries (`inherited`), as the readers already count it.
- **Not the model's.** A refund decision is never handed to the model stage.
- **No prior charge.** A card credit (or a REFUND-word credit) that links to nothing gets a **plain**
  queue decision: no category, "Looks like a refund; no earlier purchase matched." Memory or a rule
  may still file such a credit, as before.
- **Unchanged:** undo (it clears the link, as before) and idempotency.

**Merchant memory keeps `merchantSignature`.** Memory rows are stored under it.

- Dropping "credit" there would orphan every memory of "CREDIT ONE BANK" or "CREDIT KARMA".
- It would also let a learned purchase category auto-file a refund.
- So the stripped signature is used for refund linking only. This is a deliberate narrowing of the
  brief's "the merchant signature strips…", and it is listed below.

**The direction.** Spend goes down by the refund, and the room left goes up by the same amount. The card's
charges and a closed week's payoff go down by the refund. The open week's payoff holds.

**Why "never high" still holds.**

- A refund is bank-confirmed money back.
- The room it returns is real: the household spent $86.33 and got $20 back, so $66.33 counts against the
  cap. The card statement is $20 lower.
- The floors keep it from going further:
  - a refund never returns more room than its own account spent in that window;
  - a refund never nets another account or another week;
  - a closed week is never re-sized.
- The open week's payoff holds, so the curve does not move for an open-week refund. The curve rises only
  when a refund lowers what a closed week actually owes the card.
- Credits that are not clearly refunds stay out, which reads low:
  - a deposit on checking without a REFUND word;
  - anything shaped like a payment, transfer or income.

## Residuals

1. **Checking refunds need the word.** A debit-card refund that Plaid names exactly like the purchase
   (no REFUND word) is linked and queued by the categorizer, but it does not net checking spending. This
   reads low. Netting on the link alone would let a Venmo cash-in or a paycheck that happens to share a
   signature raise the room.
2. **The recap's "spent yesterday" and the Budget page's category actuals stay as they were.** The recap
   lists purchases. The Budget page already shows inflows per category beside its spend.
3. **A refund is netted by the date it is dated, not the day it arrived.** A late-posting refund
   backdated into a closed week lowers that week's charges, the true amount the card owes. If that week
   was already paid on evidence, nothing moves; if it was unpaid, its payoff is smaller by the refund.
4. **A card credit with no matching purchase** (a statement credit, an offer credit) nets the card's
   spending in its window. It is money back on the card, and the card owes that much less.
