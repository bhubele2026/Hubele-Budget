## The model rule

A language model never works out whether something is affordable. When someone asks the assistant "Can I spend
$300 this weekend?", the assistant calls the `evaluate_scenario` tool. The tool runs the same code as
`POST /money/afford` and returns the figures that code worked out. The assistant can only repeat those figures,
and the grounding check marks any number that did not come from a tool. The nightly wish-list job calls no model
at all. Nothing in this package calls a model.

## How a purchase is evaluated

1. **The curve.** The purchase is added as one outflow on its day, placed after that day's items. `walkLedger`
   then walks the ledger's own items again, from the same starting balance (`rollForwardBalance`) over the same
   90-day window.
2. **The position.** `computePosition` runs again on the baseline's exact inputs. It gets the new `daily`, the
   purchase as an `expense` event, and, when the purchase falls in this Sunday–Saturday week, the purchase as weekly
   spend. A purchase after Saturday does not change this week's cap.
3. **The date.** With no date the purchase counts today. A past date also counts today. A date after the curve's
   last day is refused with 400 `date_past_window`, because off the curve it would change nothing and read high.
4. **The category.** Planned minus spent this month minus the amount. A category with no monthly plan is shown with
   null figures and an assumption that says so. This covers bill-backed or auto categories and the system
   "Uncategorized".
5. **The verdict**, from the proposed figures:
   - `breaks_zero`: the lowest balance goes under $0.
   - `breaks_buffer`: the lowest balance goes under the buffer plus reserves. "Lowest" is the lower of the curve's
     whole-window low and the low until payday, with payday's bills counted before the paycheck.
   - `tight`: the cash holds, but this week goes over its cap, or less than 15% of what was left this week or
     until payday remains. The 15% test is done in whole cents, ×100 against ×15.
   - `fits`: none of the above.

   If the curve was already under the buffer before the purchase, the result says so.
6. **The debt rule.** The planned extra (`avalanche_settings.manual_extra`) is paid from checking. If what is left
   until payday after the purchase is **less than** one month's extra, H2 assumes the purchase is paid from that
   month's extra. The same applies when the bank balance is unknown. That month's extra is then cut by
   `min(amount, extra)`. No other month changes. The engine is not changed. `composeCutRun` runs `simulate` three
   times: the months before at the full extra, the cut month alone, then the rest at the full extra. Minimums
   freed by debts paid off along the way are passed into the next run in the engine's own order.
   `debtFreeRangeWithCut` is `debtFreeRange`'s three runs with the cut applied. A cut can never show a gain: if
   snowball's re-ordering would finish sooner or pay less interest, the baseline figure is kept and the result says
   so. The result reports `debtFreeMonthShift` (0 when unaffected) and `interestDelta` ("0.00" when unaffected).

## Worked example: the scenario household, "$300 on Saturday"

The question is asked at S4 (Wed 10/7 noon, `householdScenario.integration.test.ts`). It is stateless, so no later
step changes.

Curve: 10/7 2,075.00 · 10/8 1,980.00 (Phone −95) · 10/9 3,980.00 (Paycheck A +2,000) · 10/10 3,640.00 (Platinum
payoff −340) · 10/12 1,840.00 (Mortgage −1,800) · **10/13 1,700.00** (Electric −140) · 10/15 3,200.00 (Paycheck B).
Payday is Fri 10/9. Saturday is after it.

| Figure | Before | After $300 on Sat 10/10 | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remaining this week | 113.40 | **−186.60** | 113.40 − 300: counted against this week's $300 cap |
| Safe to spend now | 113.40 | **0.00** | max(0, min(−186.60, 1,480.00)) |
| Available until payday | 1,480.00 | 1,480.00 | the window is 10/7–10/9 and the purchase is after it |
| Lowest on the curve | 1,700.00 (10/13) | **1,400.00** (10/13) | every day from 10/10 is $300 lower |
| Debt-free range / interest | none / 0.00 | none / 0.00 | the scenario household has no debts |
| Verdict | | **tight** | the cash holds (1,400 ≥ the $500 buffer), but the week goes over its cap |

On household A (`afford.integration.test.ts`, which adds a $300 Dining plan), the same purchase reads safe now
144.50 → 0.00, week 144.50 → −155.50, until payday 2,124.50 (unchanged), lowest 2,624.50 (unchanged; 10/12 drops
to 3,124.50), Dining 274.50 → −25.50, verdict `tight`.

## Must not change, and the runs that prove it

- **`GET /money/position` is byte-identical.** `afford.integration.test.ts` checks that the response text equals
  `JSON.stringify(computePosition(baseline.positionInputs))`. `moneyPosition.integration`, `spineParity` and the
  household scenario all pass unchanged.
- **A $0 purchase reproduces the baseline to the cent.** This holds on 300 random households and on household A
  through the server read.
- **No purchase shows a better figure.** On 400 random households: no cash figure goes up, no debt-free month moves
  earlier, and no interest goes down. The debt rule fired on more than 20 of them, so the law is tested.
- **Splitting the engine changes nothing.** `composeCutRun` with nothing cut equals `simulate` on 2,000 random debt
  sets, covering both strategies, new charges, paid-off and underwater debts and split months 1–5.
  `debtFreeRangeWithCut` with nothing cut equals `debtFreeRange` on 300 more.
- **Category figures match the Budget month.** For every category measured, planned and spent equal
  `GET /budget/months/2026-10-01`'s `plannedAmount` / `actualAmount`.
- **Golden test:** the ledger golden passes under `CI=true` and is not regenerated.

## Residuals

- **Next week's cap is not read.** A purchase after Saturday leaves this week's cap alone and does not count
  against next week's, because the position only reads the current week. The assumption says so.
- **Bill-backed and auto categories have no category effect.** Their monthly plan comes from the bills or debts
  linked to them, and the Budget page derives it inline in `routes/budget.ts` (PR-E's area). This package does not
  copy that derivation. It reports "no monthly plan to measure against" instead.
- **A month never opened on the Budget page has no stored line**, so its manual categories read with no plan
  until the page seeds them.
- **`member`** is checked to belong to the household and recorded. The shared weekly cap is used for everyone,
  because per-member caps are not read by the position yet.
- **AI-2 did not have the `evaluate_scenario` tool.** The brief described it as a one-line change; it is added
  here as a new read tool, and the chat prompt is unchanged.
- **The wish-list job evaluates "as if bought today"** and ignores `target_date`, which can be past the curve.
- **The `tight` 15% compares against what was left before the purchase**, not against the cap. A $0 purchase is
  never `tight` unless the week is already over its cap.
