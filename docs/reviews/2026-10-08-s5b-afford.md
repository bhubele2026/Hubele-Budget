## Rules held

- Nothing is worked out in the browser. Verdict, both figures on every line, the debt-free months and "later by N months" (`debt.debtFreeMonthShift`) are the server's. The one comparison the page makes is on calendar dates (the weekend chip).
- Stateless: Check writes nothing. Only "Add to wish list" posts, and only when pressed. Any change to an input clears the answer, so a result never sits beside a different question.
- Status is a word first (`StatusWord`): Fits / Tight / Would dip below your buffer / Would overdraw. The verdict is the first element of the result block.
- No cents on screen; each figure keeps the cent in `<data value>`.
- The server's `tight` can come from a week over its cap while cash holds (PR-F1 note, item 5). The sheet shows the server's word and the week line shows the overrun; it does not reinterpret.

## Afford a purchase: what the person sees

1. Today. Under "This week" a quiet, full-width button: "Can we afford something?". Hover or focus starts the download of the sheet; nothing of it was on the open path.
2. Press it. The sheet comes up from the bottom on a phone and in from the right on a desktop, with the cursor in "How much".
3. Type 300. "When" offers Today, This weekend (the coming Saturday; today if it is one) and Next payday (from the position); a date field sits under the chips. A category list (budget categories, not "Uncategorized") and, only when personal allowances exist, "Whose".
4. Press Check. An empty or zero amount does not send: the field says "Enter a dollar amount above $0". Over $100,000 does not send.
5. The answer, under a rule: "$300 on Oct 10", then the verdict word, then three lines:
   - Free until payday: $2,125 → $2,125
   - This week: $145 left → -$156
   - Dining: $275 left → -$26 (only when a category was chosen)
   then "Debt-free: Mar 2027–Jun 2027 · unchanged" (or "· later by 1 month"; hidden when the household has no debt-free date), then "What this assumes" as a disclosure.
6. Add to wish list asks "What is it", then posts title, amount and category. Nothing is saved before that. Close (or Escape) returns focus to the button.
7. A date past the curve shows the server's refusal in words ("That day is further out than H2 can see").

Wish list: each pending item with an amount has "Check now". The answer shows inline: the word, "evaluated Oct 7", "free until payday after $1,645".

## Residuals and questions

1. `GET /wishlist` rows do not carry `lastEvaluation` (the PR-F1 spec adds it only to the evaluate response). The wish list therefore shows a verdict only after "Check now" in this visit. The row type is read as `WishlistItem & { lastEvaluation? }`, so the inline word lights up with no UI change as soon as the list route returns it. Needs an API change (one field on the list row) in the owner's next package.
2. The brief named a `kit/Delta`; there is none. `Delta` lives in `AffordSheet.tsx` (lazy) so the kit and the open path stay as they were. Say if you want it promoted.
3. Category is a native `<select>` rather than a `CategoryChip` picker: a long list of chips is the chip wall the owner rejected, and a native control is the phone keypad-friendly one. `CategoryChip` stays for read-only display elsewhere.
4. The open path is 399,864 of 400,000 bytes (was about 398,400). The Afford button, its prefetch call and the chunk-dependency table cost about 1.5 KB. Any further eager growth must lazy-load something else first.
5. A member's name in "Whose" comes from the member list, which only an owner may read; for anyone else the picker reads "Member 1", "Member 2".
